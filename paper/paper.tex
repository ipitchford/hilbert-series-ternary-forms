\begin{abstract}
We determine the Hilbert series of the rings of $\SL_3(\C)$-invariants of ternary forms of degrees $7$, $8$ and $9$. Within the literature we found, the largest degree previously completed was $6$ (Bedratyuk--Xin, 2011). The septic series equals the candidate $R_7(t)$ of version~0.1.0 of this work, where it was proved only through degree $760$ and conditionally beyond. The least denominator of $R_7$ has degree $1386$. \OCTICABSTRACT

The method is not new. Derksen's universal denominators bound the pole orders of the Hilbert series at roots of unity by fixed-point dimensions, computable from the torus weights. The functional equation fixes the degree of the numerator (Knop; Stanley). Finitely many exactly computed coefficients then determine the series, as in Makam's work on matrix semi-invariants. Our contribution is the application to ternary forms. We compute the torus bounds for $d=7,8,9$ by an exhaustive enumeration, which a separate program reproduces blind. We also compute the required coefficients exactly: for septics, the degree-$760$ data already suffice. The same pipeline re-derives the known series for $d=5,6$ exactly.

This version also corrects two attribution omissions in version~0.1.0.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let $I_{3,d}=\C[S^d\C^3]^{\SL_3}$ be the ring of invariants of ternary forms of degree $d$, graded by degree, and let $H_d(t)=\sum_n\dim(I_{3,d})_nt^n$. The series is classical for $d\le4$ (see~\cite{BX}). Bedratyuk and Xin~\cite{BX} computed $d=5,6$ by MacMahon partition analysis. For $d=7$, Bedratyuk~\cite{Bed08} gave the coefficients through degree $21$. We found no complete series for any $d\ge7$ (search record in \texttt{PRIOR\_ART.md}; MathSciNet and zbMATH were not searched).

Version~0.1.0 of this work~\cite{V01} gave a rational function $R_7$ and proved $H_7=R_7$ through degree $760$. Full equality was proved only under a hypothesis on a homogeneous system of parameters, whose existence was unknown.

\subsection*{Method and what is new}
The route to both theorems has four steps, and each is known.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Pole orders from fixed points.} Derksen~\cite{Der05} defines the universal denominator $\udenom(M,t)$ of a graded module $M$, the least common multiple of the denominators of the Hilbert series of all submodules. He proves that $\udenom(M,t)=\prod_r\Phi_r^{m_r}$ with $m_r=\dim M/I^{[r]}M$, where $I^{[r]}$ is generated by the homogeneous elements whose degree is not divisible by $r$~\cite[Thm.~1.10]{Der05}.
\item \emph{Reduction to the torus.} For $G$ connected reductive and $T$ a maximal torus, $\udenom(S^G)$ divides $\udenom(S^T)$~\cite[Thm.~3.4]{Der05}. The torus version is explicit: it is a maximum of $\#I-\operatorname{rank}\Omega_I$ over weight subsets $I$ satisfying a lattice condition~\cite[Thm.~5.1]{Der05}.
\item \emph{Functional equation.} The rings are Gorenstein with $\deg H_d=-\dim V$ (Knop's criterion, as used in~\cite{V01}). So $H_d\cdot B$ is a palindromic polynomial of known degree for every denominator $B$.
\item \emph{Finite determination.} Half the coefficients of that polynomial then determine $H_d$. Makam used exactly this combination of universal denominators, Knop's theorem and the functional equation for matrix semi-invariants~\cite[\S\S3,6]{Mak16}.
\end{enumerate}
Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item the exhaustive computation of the torus bounds for $S^d\C^3$ with $d=7,8,9$, reproduced by an independently written program;
\item the observation that for $d=7$ these bounds are within $2$ of the true multiplicities at every order, so that the exact data of~\cite{V01} already suffice;
\item the exact coefficients for $d=8,9$;
\item the certificates.
\end{itemize}

\section{The certificate}\label{sec:cert}

Let $G=\SL_3$, $V=S^d\C^3$, $A=\C[V]^G$ and $\delta=\dim V-8$, the Krull dimension. Write $H=H_d=\sum a_nt^n$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:cert}
Let $T\subset G$ be the diagonal torus. Write $\udenom(\C[V]^T,t)=\prod_r\Phi_r^{b_r}$, and let $B=\prod_r\Phi_r^{p_r}$ with $p_r=\min(\delta,b_r)$. Then $P=H\cdot B$ is a polynomial with $P(t)=\varepsilon\,t^KP(1/t)$, where $K=\deg B-\dim V$ and $\varepsilon=(-1)^{\delta}\cdot(-1)^{p_1}$. Consequently $H$ is determined by $a_0,\dots,a_{\lfloor K/2\rfloor}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By~\cite[Thm.~3.4]{Der05}, $\udenom(A)$ divides $\udenom(\C[V]^T)$. The pole order of $H$ at a primitive $r$-th root of unity is at most the exponent of $\Phi_r$ in $\udenom(A)$, since $A$ is a submodule of itself. So it is at most $b_r$. It is also at most $\delta$: by homogeneous Noether normalisation, $A$ is finite over a polynomial ring in $\delta$ variables. Hence $H\cdot B$ has no finite poles and is a polynomial.

The functional equation $H(1/t)=(-1)^{\delta}t^{\dim V}H(t)$ (\S\ref{sec:fe}) and $B(1/t)=(-1)^{p_1}t^{-\deg B}B(t)$ give the symmetry. A polynomial of degree at most $K$ with this symmetry is determined by its coefficients of degree at most $\lfloor K/2\rfloor$, and these are those of $H\cdot B$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The functional equation}\label{sec:fe}
The argument of~\cite[\S3.1]{V01} applies to every $d\ge5$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $A$ is Cohen--Macaulay~\cite{HR}. It is factorial because $\SL_3$ has no nontrivial characters, and hence Gorenstein~\cite{Murthy}.
\item $S^d\C^3$ is not coregular for $d\ge4$~\cite{Sch78}, so it is $2$-large~\cite[Thm.~3.7]{HS}.
\item Knop's criterion, as stated in~\cite[Thm.~3.3]{Makam}, then gives $\deg H=-\dim V$.
\end{itemize}
Stanley's theorem~\cite{Stanley} gives $H(1/t)=(-1)^\delta t^{\dim V}H(t)$. For $d=7$: $\dim V=36$ and $\delta=28$. For $d=8$: $\dim V=45$ and $\delta=37$. For $d=9$: $\dim V=55$ and $\delta=47$.

\section{The torus bounds}\label{sec:torus}

The torus weights of $S^d\C^3$ are $w=(a-c,b-c)$ for $a+b+c=d$. By~\cite[Thm.~5.1]{Der05} and the proof of~\cite[Thm.~3.4]{Der05}, $b_r$ is the largest dimension of a torus quotient $V_S/\!/T$. Here $S=\{w:\chi_w(t)=\zeta\}$ is the set of weights on which some torus element $t$ acts by a primitive $r$-th root of unity $\zeta$, and
\[
\dim V_S/\!/T=|S_0|-\operatorname{rank}S_0,\qquad S_0=\{w\in S:-w\in\operatorname{cone}(S)\}.
\]
We enumerate every such level set exactly. Write $S=W\cap\phi^{-1}(\zeta)$ for a homomorphism $\phi:\Z^2\to\C^*$.
\begin{itemize}
\item If the differences of $S$ span a rank-$2$ lattice, then $\ker\phi$ has cyclic quotient of order at most the largest $|\det|$ of two weight differences ($147$, $192$ and $243$ for $d=7,8,9$). All such kernels are enumerated.
\item If $S$ lies on a line, then $\dim V_S/\!/T=0$ unless the line passes through $0$. In that case $S$ lies in a residue class of multiples of a primitive direction, and these are enumerated up to the diameter of the line.
\item Singletons contribute $0$.
\end{itemize}
The program is \texttt{scripts/pole\_bounds.py}. A second program, written blind by a separate agent from the definition alone, enumerates the lattice condition of~\cite[Thm.~5.1]{Der05} directly. It gives identical values of $b_r$ for $d=5,\dots,9$, and for $d\le7$ it found an explicit torsion witness for every value. For the known cases $d=5,6$, the bounds are at least the true multiplicities of the least denominator everywhere, and positive exactly on its pole set.

\section{Plane septics: proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sept}}

For $d=7$ the bound $B=\prod\Phi_r^{p_r}$ has degree $1452$, so $K=1416$ and $\varepsilon=+1$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}, $H_7$ is determined by $a_0,\dots,a_{708}$. These are known as exact integers for $n\le760$~\cite{V01}. They come from a weight-counting computation modulo $15$ distinct $16$-bit primes, which the package regenerates from scratch in about three minutes.

The script \texttt{certify\_septic\_unconditional.py} checks, in exact integer arithmetic, that:
\begin{itemize}
\item the least denominator $D_7$ of $R_7$ divides $B$;
\item $Q=R_7B$ is a polynomial of degree at most $1416$ with $Q(t)=t^{1416}Q(1/t)$;
\item the series of $R_7$ equals the exact $a_n$ for every $n\le708$.
\end{itemize}
The script regenerates the torus bounds itself. Hence $H_7B$ and $Q$ are reciprocal polynomials of degree $1416$ that agree in their lower halves, so they are equal, and $H_7=R_7$.

The bound exceeds the true multiplicity only at $r\in\{2,4,5,6,9,12,15,18\}$, and by exactly $2$ each time. The same pattern occurs for $d=5,6$. Presumably the bound becomes exact if centralisers larger than $T$ are used in the proof of~\cite[Thm.~3.4]{Der05}; we do not need this.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item The theorems are computer-assisted. They rest on the cited theorems, on the enumeration programs, and on the correctness of the coefficient computations: for septics, exact weight counting; for octics, \OCTICLIMIT.
\item No step is formally verified.
\item Knop's and Formanek's theorems are used through the restatements in~\cite{Makam,Ber}.
\item All review is model-based.
\item The next case, $d=10$, needs exact coefficients up to degree $3249$ with the torus bound, roughly $100$ CPU-hours with the present engine. The excess of the torus bound over the truth, always $2$ and at a few small orders, suggests that sharper bounds from larger centralisers would reduce this.
\end{itemize}
